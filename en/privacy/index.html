Privacy

Updated on October 23, 2026.

Wayout is a Windows desktop app that measures and cleans the junk software development leaves behind. It runs on your computer, has no accounts and asks for no personal data. This page lists everything it stores on your computer and every connection it makes.

What it scans stays on your computer

To measure your drive, Wayout reads folder paths, sizes and dates, the names of your projects, and the list of running processes and open ports. All of it is shown in the window and Wayout sends none of it to any server.

What it stores on your computer

  • Settings, in %APPDATA%\pe.avanc3.wayout\settings.json: whether the background check is on, the notification threshold, the language, the last measured figure, the folders it searches for projects and your answer about anonymous statistics.
  • Window preferences (language, chosen folders, whether you saw the tour) and the last copy of the ads file, in the window's local storage inside %LOCALAPPDATA%\pe.avanc3.wayout.
  • The clean-up log, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Limpiador\limpieza.log: every deleted path with its size, the method (Recycle Bin or permanent delete) and the date.
  • The error log, errores.log, next to the clean-up log: technical messages from the app itself when something fails, in case you want to send them when reporting a problem. It stays on your computer and Wayout never sends it. If it is over 512 KB when Wayout opens, it is kept as errores.log.old and a new one starts.
  • If you turn on "Open Wayout when you sign in to Windows", an entry in HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run, removed when you turn it off.

To remove all of this, uninstall Wayout and delete those folders.

The connections it makes

  • When it opens, it downloads https://wayout.avanc3.pe/sponsor.json, the file with the ads it shows. It is hosted on GitHub Pages. As with any website visit, the server receives your IP address and technical details of the request; the request carries no data about you or your computer.
  • After measuring, it checks https://github.com/kasbsquall/wayout-releases/releases/latest/download/latest.json to find out whether a new version exists, at most once every 12 hours while the app is open. GitHub receives your IP address and technical details of the request; the request carries no data about you or your computer. If there is a new version, Wayout downloads and installs it only when you ask, and checks its signature first.
  • If you accept anonymous statistics, it sends to eu.aptabase.com what the next section describes.
  • Banner images are served from this same site (wayout.avanc3.pe) and not from the advertiser's, so no advertiser receives your IP address unless you click.
  • Clicking an ad or a help link opens it in your browser. Advertiser links may be affiliate links: they tell the advertiser the visit came from Wayout so it pays a commission if you buy, at no extra cost to you.

Anonymous statistics, only if you accept

After your first scan, Wayout asks whether you want to share anonymous statistics. Nothing is sent until you accept, and you can change your answer any time in Settings. They tell us which tools to add to the catalog.

If you accept, after each scan and each clean-up you run in the window it sends:

  • Which tools and folder types it found, only by their catalog name (for example npm, cargo or node_modules), how many GB each one takes and the total there was to free up.
  • For a clean-up, the GB freed, how many items were deleted and which blocks they came from (caches, projects or temp files).
  • The Wayout, Windows, WebView2 and Aptabase component versions, the Windows language and region (for example es-PE), the time of each event and a random session identifier that changes after 4 hours without use.

It never sends paths, project names, your user name or the contents of your files. The background check sends nothing.

The statistics go to Aptabase, a service of Technov Solutions SRL (Romania) acting as data processor, in its European Union region (Germany). According to its privacy policy (updated July 24, 2026), it uses your IP address to compute an identifier that changes every day, without storing the IP, and it keeps the data for up to 5 years. According to its code, which is open source, it also records the approximate country and region the connection comes from (for example, Peru and Lima). Since nothing that is sent identifies you, neither Wayout nor Aptabase can find your data to delete it; to stop sending it, turn the option off in Settings.

The background check

If you turn it on, every 8 hours Wayout measures caches, project folders and temp files on your computer and, if they go over the threshold you chose, shows a Windows notification. The check deletes nothing and sends nothing.

If you connect it to an AI assistant

Wayout includes an MCP server for coding assistants such as Claude Code. It does nothing on its own: it only works if you register it in your assistant. When the assistant queries it, it hands over the paths, sizes, dates and project names it measures, and the assistant may send them to the AI service you use, under that service's privacy rules. The server deletes nothing, shows no ads and makes no connections of its own.

If you installed it from the Microsoft Store

The Microsoft Store version stores the same things and makes the same connections, with three differences:

  • Everything it stores stays inside the package folder, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages: settings and the clean-up log go in its LocalState folder, whose exact path Settings and the clean-up log show. Windows deletes all of it when you uninstall.
  • It does not check GitHub for new versions: updates come through the Store.
  • "Open Wayout when you sign in to Windows" is a Windows startup task instead of the registry entry. You can also see and change it in Task Manager, under Startup apps.

Downloading and installing from the Store are covered by Microsoft's terms and privacy policy.
